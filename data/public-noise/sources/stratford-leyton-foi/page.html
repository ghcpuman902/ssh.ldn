FOI request detail

Central Line Noise Between Stratford and Leyton

Request ID: FOI-3133-2526
Date published: 09 December 2025

You asked
1. Rail maintenance and grinding history a) Dates, exact locations (chainage or track reference), and scope of all rail grinding, replacement, or renewal works carried out between Stratford and Leyton (eastbound) since January 2020. b) Corresponding pre- and post-intervention decibel and/or vibration data, as measured in accordance with TfL’s internal noise monitoring procedure (e.g. as referenced on TfL’s “Reducing Noise and Vibration” webpage, updated 2024). c) Copies of any inspection reports or maintenance logs generated as part of those interventions. 2. Noise and vibration monitoring data a) The most recent noise and vibration measurements for this section (both eastbound running tunnel and platforms if applicable), including: i. Measured dB(A) levels; ii. Measurement duration and equipment used; iii. Location or mileage reference of measurement points; and iv. Comparative data from other Central line sections. b) TfL’s current internal criteria or thresholds for defining when a section of rail requires attention due to noise, corrugation, or vibration exceedance. 3. Track form and fastening systems a) Details of any modern or trialled rail fastening systems or under-rail damping installations applied between Stratford and Leyton, including those referenced in press coverage of TfL’s tests of “Delkor” or similar resilient fastenings (see Timeout, August 2022; TfL statement on rail-noise technology). b) Any performance reports, internal evaluations, or supplier test data relevant to these installations. 4. Planned works and noise-mitigation programmes a) The planned schedule of rail grinding, replacement, or major track renewals between Stratford and Leyton for the period 2024–2026. b) Copies of any internal correspondence, meeting minutes, or engineering reports since January 2023 in which the Stratford–Leyton eastbound section is described as a “noise hotspot” or is prioritised for mitigation. c) Any budget allocation or project references relating specifically to noise mitigation on this section (for example, under the £150 million annual track improvement and £1 million noise-technology budgets cited on TfL’s website). 5. Noise-mapping and comparative analysis a) The most recent network-wide noise map or comparative dataset showing where the Stratford–Leyton eastbound section ranks against other Central line segments in measured dB(A) levels. b) Copies of any reports, presentations, or external communications since January 2023 that include this section in the context of rail-noise management.

We answered

TfL Ref: FOI-3133-2526

 

Thank you for your request which we received on 13 November 2025, asking for information about noise on the Central line.

 

Your request has been considered in accordance with the requirements of the Environmental Information Regulations and our information access policy. I can confirm that we hold some of the information you require. 

 

We recognise that the noise on some parts of TfL’s services can be high and uncomfortable at times. We measure the volume of noise, and the duration of this noise, across all the London Underground lines. For the majority of our customers, exposure to very high levels of noise on our services is usually for short periods of time. While there are no national standards for public exposure to noise, there are legally set levels of noise for employees. We monitor the health risks relating to noise exposure for our colleagues who spend most time travelling on the network, e.g. train operators. This information, which confirms that train operators’ exposure to noise while driving trains during their shifts is within the legal requirements set by the Health & Safety Executive, gives us confidence that it is unlikely that our customers – who have much lower exposure to noise – are at significant health risks/loss of hearing risk as a result of the noise on our network. 

 

We are considering, given there is no national legal framework for exposure to noise for the public or customers, how best to measure that noise to ensure that we do not expose our customers to health risks associated with high levels of noise. 

 

We continue to invest significantly in track renewal and maintenance, including a continuous programme of rail grinding and track modernisation. We have undertaken a significant amount of noise and vibration-related rail grinding in the last six months and will continue to carry out other targeted interventions to reduce noise. In addition to this, TfL also uses lubrication to manage friction between the wheels and rails.

 

You specifically asked for: 

 

1. Rail maintenance and grinding history 

  1. Dates, exact locations (chainage or track reference), and scope of all rail grinding, replacement, or renewal works carried out between Stratford and Leyton (eastbound) since January 2020.
  2. Corresponding pre- and post-intervention decibel and/or vibration data, as measured in accordance with TfL’s internal noise monitoring procedure (e.g. as referenced on TfL’s “Reducing Noise and Vibration” webpage, updated 2024).
  3. Copies of any inspection reports or maintenance logs generated as part of those interventions.

 

2. Noise and vibration monitoring data  

a) The most recent noise and vibration measurements for this section (both eastbound running tunnel and platforms if applicable), including: i. Measured dB(A) levels;

ii. Measurement duration and equipment used;

iii. Location or mileage reference of measurement points; and

iv. Comparative data from other Central line sections.

b) TfL’s current internal criteria or thresholds for defining when a section of rail requires attention due to noise, corrugation, or vibration exceedance. 

 

3. Track form and fastening systems 

a) Details of any modern or trialled rail fastening systems or under-rail damping installations applied between Stratford and Leyton, including those referenced in press coverage of TfL’s tests of “Delkor” or similar resilient fastenings (see Timeout, August 2022; TfL statement on rail-noise technology).

b) Any performance reports, internal evaluations, or supplier test data relevant to these installations.

 

4. Planned works and noise-mitigation programmes

a) The planned schedule of rail grinding, replacement, or major track renewals between Stratford and Leyton for the period 2024–2026. 

b) Copies of any internal correspondence, meeting minutes, or engineering reports since January 2023 in which the Stratford–Leyton eastbound section is described as a “noise hotspot” or is prioritised for mitigation.

c) Any budget allocation or project references relating specifically to noise mitigation on this section (for example, under the £150 million annual track improvement and £1 million noise-technology budgets cited on TfL’s website).

 

5. Noise-mapping and comparative analysis

  1. The most recent network-wide noise map or comparative dataset showing where the Stratford–Leyton eastbound section ranks against other Central line segments in measured dB(A) levels.
  2. Copies of any reports, presentations, or external communications since January 2023 that include this section in the context of rail-noise management.

Please note that in accordance with TfL’s obligations under the Data Protection legislation some personal data has been removed, as required by Regulation 13 of the Environmental Information Regulations. This is because disclosure of this personal data would be a breach of the legislation, specifically the first principle of the legislation, which requires all processing of personal data to be fair and lawful. It would not be fair to disclose this personal information when the individuals have no expectation it would be disclosed and TfL has not satisfied one of the conditions of Schedule 2 which would make the processing ‘fair’.

 

Unfortunately, given the extent of the information you are looking for across  your request, we are applying Regulation 12(4)(b) ‘manifestly unreasonable’ because providing all of the information you have requested would impose unreasonable costs on us and require an unreasonable diversion of resources. We do wish to clarify that whilst we consider that your request falls under regulation 12(4)(b), this does not reflect a conclusion that it has been your intention to deliberately place an undue burden on our resources.

When we receive requests asking for correspondence and communications, we are able to conduct a companywide email search using keywords, dates and email addresses. The more specific a requester can be, the more we can narrow the search and therefore stand a better chance of a more relevant or focused result. A search will then return an amount of ‘hits’ which potentially contain information relating to the search terms used. We would then need to manually review all of the emails identified by the search in order to extract and collate the relevant information and disregard duplicates and unrelated material. In addition to the time taken to search and review emails, we would also need to spend time on locating and collating all the different reports and presentation notes you asked for, which would be held across several teams. We therefore consider that it would require a significant amount of staff time and resources to identify and locate all the information you asked for.  

 

The use of this exception is subject to a public interest test, which requires us to consider whether the public interest in applying the exception outweighs the public interest in disclosure. We recognise that the release of information would promote accountability and transparency in public services and also help address any concerns you may have. However, the time it would take to provide the information you have requested would divert a disproportionate amount of our resources from its core functions and on balance we consider that the public interest currently favours the use of the exception.

 

We will consider your request again, if you are able to narrow its scope so that we can more easily locate, retrieve and extract the information you are seeking. If you could ask specific questions, we will be happy to consider those. 

 

Please see the attached information sheet for details of your right to appeal as well as information on copyright and what to do if you would like to re-use any of the information we have disclosed.
