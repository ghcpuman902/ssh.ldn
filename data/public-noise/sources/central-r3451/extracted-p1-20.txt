1.0 Introduction
The Transport for London Technical Services Team are obtaining data to understand the typical interior noise levels from
services on the London Underground (LU) network. These measurements are carried on specially modified trains equipped
with Auto Visual Inspection (AVI) equipment and/or Asset Conditioning Monitoring (ACM) equipment.
The purpose of the measurements is to characterise and monitor the noise exposure of passengers given the current track
conditions whilst travelling on the LU network. This report does not assess, evaluate or make any recommendations
regarding the noise levels or whether any remedial actions need to take place.

A glossary of acoustic terms and their meanings has been included in Appendix A.
2.0 Test Methodology

The acoustic tests were conducted in accordance with BS EN ISO 2021:3381 - Railway applications — Acoustics — Noise
measurement inside railbound vehicles . It is noted that the scope within this standard " does not include all the instructions
to carry out monitoring testing or evaluation of noise exposure of passengers or drivers over a whole journey" .

2.1   Measurement Procedure
On 16/04/2025 acoustic measurement equipment was installed in the middle passenger saloon car of a LU 1992 stock train
on the Central line. The middle car was chosen as the leading carriage of the train changes depending on the route taken on
the test day, therefore with the measurement equipment in the centre of the train, the position remains constant regardless of
direction of travel.

Sound pressure data were acquired using a four channel integrating sound level meter with combined 1/2" microphone and
preamplifier sets. The following parameters were obtained over 1-second integration periods:
   - A-weighted equivalent continuous sound pressure level (LAeq,T),
   - C-weighted peak sound pressure level (LCPeak); and
   - one-third octave band unweighted equivalent continuous sound pressure level (LZeq,T) for centre band
     frequencies 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

Measurement positions were chosen to represent both seated and standing positions within the saloon. Microphones were
positioned using clamps and/or tripods at a relative height of 1.2 m and 1.6 m for seating and standing positions,
respectively. All positions were more than 0.3 m distance from any reflecting surface.

at the time of the test, not all locations were utilised. Table 1 shows the equipment used at the relevant position.

2.2   Equipment
Field calibration of the operation channels of sound level meter were performed using an acoustic calibrator which gave a
sinusoidal calibration tone of 94 dB at a frequency of 1000 Hz. The field calibration was carried out before and after the
survey; the measurements remained stable throughout. The SLM and acoustic calibrator conform to ‘class 1’ specifications
for corresponding equipment defined in British Standards BS EN 61672-1:2013, BS EN 61260-3:2016 and BS EN
60942:2018. The SLM, microphones and pre-amps have been calibrated to traceable standards within the preceding two
years, and the acoustic calibrators within the preceding 12 months.

2.3   Test Conditions
The test operating conditions for the vehicle are representative of normal service and may have included passenger
announcements. Microphones were placed away from any speakers to minimise the influence. Windows and ventilation
grilles remained closed during the exercise. Test engineers may have also accessed the carriage during the measurement
and as a result there may be some influence from doors slamming or localised speech.
Generally, the services would follow the Driving Technique Guidance where possible, however, invariably there may be
deviations or significant changes to the speed due to existing operational issues on the day of the test run, these should
therefore be used with caution.
Presented within Appendix B, is the test run order, the corresponding track Location Coding System (LCS), the direction of
travel, the interstation section station code and the interstation section description.

2.4   Tachometer
A tachometer was fitted to an axle-box on train. A signal from the tachometer was obtained and synchronised with the sound
level meter in the middle carriage. Speed and distance were derived allowing for an approximate location along each
interstation section.

2.5   Limitations
Depending on the direction of the leading car, the noise levels in the middle carriage could be +/- 70 m of the stated
measurement position along with the potentially distance covered over the 1 second integration period (i.e. speed of 20 m/s
would mean the train could be +/- 10 m over the integration period).

3.0 Analysis
A spatial average of the LAeq and the highest LCPeak measured was derived from the positions listed in Table 1. For each
interstation section, which includes the departure from the station and arrival into the next station, the following parameters
were determined: the A-weighted equivalent continuous sound pressure level (LAeq), the C-weighted peak sound pressure
level (LCPeak), the sound exposure level (SEL); and the Daily Personal Exposure level (LEP,d) as defined by the Control of
Noise at Work Regulations 2005. Additionally, one-third octave band unweighted equivalent continuous sound pressure level
(LZeq) for centre band frequencies 20 Hz to 20 kHz were processed but not provided within the report.

Appendix A - Terminology

A-weighting
    a frequency weighting devised to attempt to take into account the fact that human response to sound is not equally
    senstive to all frequencies

C-weighting
    a frequency weighting that correlates better with the human response to high noise levels and therefore noise induced
    hearing loss.

Continuous equivalent A-weighted sound pressure level, L Aeq
    the steady noise level which, over the period of time under consideration, contains the same amount of A-weighted
    sound energy as the time-varying noise, over the same period of time.

Daily personal noise exposure (L EP'd )
    the time-averaged, A-weighted noise level for a nominal 8-hour working day used for assessing the noise exposure of a
    worker during a working day.

Maximum A-weighted sound pressure level, L AFMax
    the maximum RMS A-weighted sound pressure level occuring within a specified time period with a fast time weighting.

Peak sound pressure level (L CPeak )
    the maximum instantaneous C-weighted sound pressure level within a specified time period.

Sound exposure level, SEL (L AE )
    the A-weighted sound pressure level which, if occuring over a period of one second, would contain the same amount of
    A-weighted sound energy as the event.
